## 1. Deterministic construction of finite fields

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "Deterministic polynomial factorization over prime fields" (family 142), which depends on Theorem 1.2 of the companion paper of family 029 (a uniform zero-free strip for finite-order Hecke L-functions of cyclotomic fields containing the 12th roots of unity): there is a deterministic algorithm which, given a prime p in binary and an integer n ≥ 1 in unary, outputs a monic irreducible polynomial g of degree n in 𝔽_p[x] using a number of bit operations bounded by a polynomial in n and ⌈log₂ p⌉, with an absolute exponent. The algorithm uses no randomness, no GRH, and no integer-factorization or primitive-root oracle. Consequently the finite field 𝔽_{p^n} = 𝔽_p[x]/(g) can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in n and log p, for every prime p and every degree n.

**Release input.** Family 142, "Deterministic polynomial factorization over prime fields" (OpenAI, October 4, 2026), Theorem 1.1: a uniform deterministic algorithm that, given a prime p in binary and a nonzero dense polynomial f ∈ 𝔽_p[x] of degree n, outputs the leading coefficient and the distinct monic irreducible factors of f with multiplicities in a number of bit operations polynomial in (n + 1)⌈log₂ p⌉, with absolute implied constants and without randomness, integer-factorization oracle, primitive-root oracle or GRH. Its proof uses Theorem 1.2 of the family-029 paper "Primitive roots for every admissible integer base" (OpenAI, October 4, 2026): for every finite-order Hecke character η of a cyclotomic number field F containing μ₁₂, the function L_F(s, η) has no zeros in Re s > 1 − 10^(−6). No Lean formalization is listed for families 142 and 029.

**Bridge.** Victor Shoup, "New algorithms for finding irreducible polynomials over finite fields", Math. Comp. 54 (1990), no. 189, 435–447, Theorem 3.1 (numbering of the author's manuscript): for F = GF(p), p prime, constructing an irreducible polynomial of degree n over F can be reduced deterministically, in time bounded by a polynomial in n and log p, to factoring polynomials over F. For comparison, deterministic constructions in time polynomial in n and log p under GRH are due to Adleman–Lenstra (1986) and Evdokimov (1986), a deterministic construction in time polynomial in p is in Shoup (1990), and a pseudo-deterministic construction is due to Rai (arXiv:2410.04071).

**Deduction.** Let L = ⌈log₂ p⌉. Shoup's algorithm is deterministic and runs in time polynomial in n and L apart from calls that factor polynomials over 𝔽_p. Each call is on a dense polynomial of degree at most n² over the same field 𝔽_p (a cyclotomic polynomial of degree q − 1 for each prime q | n with q ≠ p, and at most m log p polynomials of degree mq with m < q), and the number of calls is polynomial in n and L. Replacing each call by the algorithm of Theorem 1.1, which factors every dense polynomial over 𝔽_p for every prime p given in binary, gives a total running time polynomial in n and L. The cases q = 2 and q = p are handled in Shoup's Theorem 2.1 without factoring, at cost polynomial in n. The output has nL bits, so a bound polynomial in n (unary) and log p is the natural form. The direction used is: factoring over 𝔽_p in polynomial time implies construction of irreducible polynomials over 𝔽_p in polynomial time.

**Scope.** The statement concerns irreducible polynomials of every degree n over a prime field 𝔽_p, with p given in binary and the running time measured in n (unary) and log p.

## 2. Lower bounds for Carmichael and Novak–Carmichael numbers

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "The Poisson–Dirichlet law for prime predecessors" (family 011). Let C(x) be the number of Carmichael numbers ≤ x, and let N_C(x) be the number of Novak–Carmichael numbers ≤ x, i.e. positive integers n with a^n ≡ 1 (mod n) for every a coprime to n, equivalently p − 1 | n for every prime p | n (terminology of Kalmynin).

- (a) For every fixed ε > 0 there is x₀(ε) with C(x) ≥ x^(5/12 − ε) for all x ≥ x₀ (Alford–Granville–Pomerance, Theorem 1, with E ∈ (0,1) arbitrary and B < 5/12).
- (a′) With the value B = 0.4736 of Harman (Int. J. Number Theory 4 (2008) 241–248), as recorded in Wright (Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. 101 (2020), Theorem 2.3 and p. 381) and Lichtman (arXiv:2211.09641): C(x) ≥ x^(0.4736 − ε) for all x ≥ x₀(ε).
- (b) For every fixed ε > 0, N_C(x) ≥ x^(1 − ε) for x ≥ x₀(ε) (Kalmynin, Theorem 1); hence N_C(x) = x^(1 − o(1)).

**Release input.** Family 011, "The Poisson–Dirichlet law for prime predecessors" (OpenAI, September 24, 2026), Theorem 1.1: for fixed k ≥ 1 and bounded continuous F : [0,1]^k → ℝ, the average of F(V₁(p), …, V_k(p)) over the primes 3 ≤ p ≤ x, with equal weights, tends to E F(L₁, …, L_k) as x → ∞ through all real x. Here V_j(p) = log q_j(p) / log(p − 1), q₁ ≥ q₂ ≥ … are the prime factors of p − 1 counted with multiplicity, and (L_j) has the Poisson–Dirichlet law PD(1). The paper records that this resolves the conjecture of Granville (its (1.3)): #{p ≤ x : P⁺(p − 1) ≤ x^(1/u)} ∼ ρ(u) π(x) for fixed u ≥ 1, where P⁺(m) is the largest prime factor of m and ρ is Dickman's function. The companion paper "Weighted dilation graphs, smooth shifted primes and totient fibers" (family 011), Theorem 1.2, gives #{p prime : 2x < p ≤ 5x, P⁺(p − 1) ≤ x^δ} ≥ x^(1 − o(1)) for fixed 0 < δ < 1/4. No Lean formalization is listed for family 011.

**Bridge.**
- W. R. Alford, A. Granville, C. Pomerance, "There are infinitely many Carmichael numbers", Ann. of Math. (2) 139 (1994) 703–722. Let π(x, y) count the primes p ≤ x such that p − 1 has no prime factor exceeding y. Let 𝓔 be the set of E ∈ (0,1) for which π(x, x^(1−E)) ≥ γ(E) π(x) for some γ(E) > 0 and all large x (condition (0.1)); Erdős conjectured 𝓔 = (0,1). Let 𝓑 be the set of B ∈ (0,1) for which there are x₂(B) and a positive integer D_B such that, for x ≥ x₂(B), π(y; d, a) ≥ π(y)/(2φ(d)) whenever (a,d) = 1, d ≤ min{x^B, y/x^(1−B)} and d is divisible by none of at most D_B exceptional integers, each exceeding log x (condition (0.3)). Theorem 2.1: (0, 5/12) ⊂ 𝓑. Theorem 1: for E ∈ 𝓔 and B ∈ 𝓑 there is x₀(E, B) with C(x) ≥ x^(EB) for x ≥ x₀.
- G. Harman, "Watt's mean value theorem and Carmichael numbers", Int. J. Number Theory 4 (2008) 241–248: the admissible value B = 0.4736.
- A. Kalmynin, "Novak–Carmichael numbers and shifted primes without large prime factors", arXiv:1706.07343, Theorem 1: for fixed u ∈ (0,1), if Π(z, z^u) = z^(1+o(1)) as z → ∞, then N_C(x) ≫ x^(1−u+o(1)); here Π(x, y) counts the primes p ≤ x whose predecessor p − 1 has largest prime factor at most y.

**Deduction.**
- Step 1 (𝓔 = (0,1)). Fix E ∈ (0,1) and put u = 1/(1 − E). Then π(x, x^(1−E)) = #{p ≤ x : P⁺(p − 1) ≤ x^(1/u)}, the quantity in (1.3), so π(x, x^(1−E)) ∼ ρ(u) π(x) with ρ(u) > 0, and (0.1) holds with γ(E) = ρ(u)/2. (From Theorem 1.1: for εx < p ≤ x one has log(p − 1)/log x = 1 − O_ε(1/log x), the event is V₁(p) ≤ 1/u + o(1), and L₁ has a continuous law with P(L₁ ≤ 1/u) = ρ(u); sandwich with continuous test functions and let ε → 0.) This is Erdős's conjecture 𝓔 = (0,1) in the formulation of Alford–Granville–Pomerance (p. 704).
- Step 2 ((a), (a′)). With B < 5/12, so B ∈ 𝓑, Theorem 1 gives C(x) ≥ x^(EB). Given ε > 0, choose E < 1 and B < 5/12 with EB > 5/12 − ε. With the admissible value B = 0.4736 the same argument gives (a′). The positive proportion γ(E) π(x) in (0.1) is the form of input that the construction of Alford–Granville–Pomerance uses.
- Step 3 ((b)). For fixed u ∈ (0,1), (1.3) gives Π(z, z^u) ∼ ρ(1/u) π(z) = z^(1+o(1)), which is Kalmynin's hypothesis; it also follows from Theorem 1.2 of the companion paper (take x = z/5 and δ = min(u, 1/5)). Kalmynin's Theorem 1 gives N_C(x) ≫ x^(1−u+o(1)); taking u = ε/2 gives N_C(x) ≥ x^(1−ε), and N_C(x) ≤ x. The smoothness conventions agree, and both results count all primes p ≤ x.

**Scope.** The exponents in (a) and (a′) are those of the progression input B of Alford–Granville–Pomerance (5/12, respectively 0.4736); the statement concerns the bounds C(x) ≥ x^(B − ε) and N_C(x) ≥ x^(1 − ε) as written.

## 3. Tate, Beilinson–Parshin and Lichtenbaum conjectures for varieties of abelian type over finite fields

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties" (family 032; the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every complex CM abelian variety), through its consequence Corollary 8.3 (the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields). Let k be a finite field of characteristic p and let X be a smooth projective k-variety of abelian type in the sense of Kahn, i.e. its Chow motive h(X) with ℚ-coefficients lies in the thick rigid subcategory A_ab of Chow motives generated by the motives h(A_L) of abelian varieties A_L over finite extensions L/k (this class contains every abelian variety over k and every product of curves). Then X lies in Kahn's class B_tate(k): the Tate conjecture holds for X in every codimension and for every prime ℓ ≠ p, i.e. the cycle class map CH^i(X) ⊗ ℚ_ℓ → H^(2i)(X̄, ℚ_ℓ(i))^(Gal(k̄/k)) is surjective for all i. Consequently, by Kahn (Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 36 (2003) 977–1002):

- (1) rational and numerical equivalence coincide on X with rational coefficients (Théorème 1.10, equivalently Théorème 1 of the introduction);
- (2) the Beilinson–Parshin conjecture holds for X: K_i(X) is a torsion group for every i > 0 (Corollaire 2.2);
- (3) the conjectures of Lichtenbaum (Lecture Notes in Math. 1068 (1984) 127–138, section 7), in the eight-item form recalled by Kahn and including the special-value formula, hold for X (Corollaire 3.10, using Corollaire 3.8);
- (4) for every open U ⊂ X, the groups H⁰(U, K₂), H¹(U, K₂) and H²(U, K₂) = CH²(U) are finitely generated, and K₃(F)\_ind = K₃(k) for the function field F of X (Théorème 4.5).

**Release input.** Family 032, "The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties" (OpenAI, September 30, 2026). Theorem 1.1: for a complex CM abelian variety A (End(A) ⊗ ℚ contains a commutative semisimple ℚ-algebra of dimension 2 dim A) and every integer j ≥ 0, the Betti cycle class map CH^j(A)\_ℚ → H^(2j)(A, ℚ) ∩ H^(j,j)(A) is surjective. Corollary 8.3 (Section 8.2): for every abelian variety A over 𝔽_q, every r ≥ 0 and every prime ℓ ≠ char 𝔽_q, the map CH^r(A) ⊗\_ℤ ℚ_ℓ → H^(2r)\_ét(Ā, ℚ_ℓ(r))^(G_q) is surjective, and numerical and ℓ-adic homological equivalence coincide on rational algebraic cycles on Ā. It is deduced from Milne's theorem (Compositio Math. 117 (1999), Theorem 7.1 and formulation (0.1)) with Theorem 1.1 as hypothesis, followed by descent from 𝔽_{q^m} to 𝔽_q. No Lean formalization is listed for this paper.

**Bridge.** Bruno Kahn, "Équivalences rationnelle et numérique sur certaines variétés de type abélien sur un corps fini", Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 36 (2003), no. 6, 977–1002 (journal numbering). Définition 1: B(k) is the set of isomorphism classes of smooth projective k-varieties whose Chow motive lies in the thick rigid subcategory A_ab generated by Artin motives and motives of abelian varieties (Notation 1.2: the smallest thick subcategory of Chow motives containing the h(A_L), L/k finite); X is of abelian type if X ∈ B(k); B_tate(k) consists of the X ∈ B(k) satisfying the Tate conjecture, for one ℓ ≠ p equivalently for all (Tate, Théorème 2.9; Kahn, Lemme 1.9). Théorème 1.10: if X ×\_k X′ ∈ B_tate(k), then CH^(dim X + n)(X′ × X) ⊗ ℚ → A_num^(dim X + n)(X′ × X) ⊗ ℚ is an isomorphism for all n ∈ ℤ. Corollaire 2.2 (building on Geisser, K-Theory 13 (1998) 109–122, Theorem 3.3), Corollaire 3.10 and Théorème 4.5 are as stated above. Kahn's Example 1(c) records Milne's implication from the Hodge conjecture for complex CM abelian varieties to the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields (see also Milne, "The Tate conjecture over finite fields", Theorem 7.4).

**Deduction.**
- (i) B_tate(k) = B(k). Let X ∈ B(k). Then h(X) is a direct summand, in Chow motives over k with ℚ-coefficients, of a finite sum of motives h(A_j)(n_j), with A_j an abelian variety over a finite extension L_j/k and n_j ∈ ℤ. Cycle class maps commute with correspondences, so the Tate map of X in codimension i is a retract of the Tate map of the sum. For h(A_L)(n) one has CH^i(A_L) = CH^i of A over L and H^(2i)(Ā_L)^(G_k) = H^(2i)(Ā)^(G_L), so the Tate map is that of the abelian variety A over the finite field L in codimension i − n (both sides vanish outside the range 0 to dim A). It is surjective by Corollary 8.3, which covers every abelian variety over every finite field, every r ≥ 0 and every ℓ ≠ p. Hence X ∈ B_tate(k).
- (ii) Théorème 1.10 with X′ = Spec k gives (1); Corollaire 2.2, Corollaire 3.10 (via Corollaire 3.8) and Théorème 4.5 apply with the hypothesis X ∈ B_tate(k).
- (iii) Hypotheses match: k is a finite field (Corollary 8.3 covers every 𝔽_q); the equivalence relations carry rational coefficients; the Tate conjecture is the surjectivity of the ℓ-adic cycle class map onto Galois invariants of H^(2i)(X̄, ℚ_ℓ(i)), with the same normalization in Corollary 8.3; no restriction on dim X, p or ℓ ≠ p enters. The descent step of Corollary 8.3 shows that the cycles are defined over 𝔽_q itself, as the definition of B_tate(k) requires.

**Scope.** The statement concerns smooth projective varieties of abelian type over a finite field, the Tate conjecture for ℓ ≠ p, and conclusions (1)–(4) exactly as in Kahn's Théorème 1.10, Corollaire 2.2, Corollaire 3.10 and Théorème 4.5.

## 4. Failure of Tachikawa's first conjecture

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "A counterexample to Tachikawa's second conjecture" (family 199). Let k = 𝔽₂(q, H₁, H₂) with q, H₁, H₂ algebraically independent, and let K be any field extension of k (K = k included). Let A be the finite-dimensional symmetric k-algebra of the release and M its finite-dimensional non-projective left module with Ext^i_A(M, M) = 0 for all i > 0, and put B_K = End_{A_K}(A_K ⊕ M_K), where A_K = K ⊗\_k A and M_K = K ⊗\_k M. Then B_K is a finite-dimensional K-algebra with Ext^i_{B_K}(DB_K, B_K) = 0 for every i > 0 (D = Hom_K(−, K)) which is not self-injective. Consequently Tachikawa's first conjecture (Tachikawa, Lecture Notes in Math. 351 (1973), Section 8: if Ext^i_A(DA, A) = 0 for all i > 0, then A is self-injective) is false for finite-dimensional algebras over K, in particular over an algebraic closure of k. The classical form of the hypothesis, Ext^i_{A^e}(A, A^e) = 0 for i > 0, is the same condition, since Ext^n_{A^e}(A, A ⊗ A) ≅ Ext^n_A(DA, A) (see the deduction).

**Release input.** Family 199, "A counterexample to Tachikawa's second conjecture" (OpenAI, September 23, 2026), Theorem 1.1: for k = 𝔽₂(q, H₁, H₂) with q, H₁, H₂ algebraically independent, there exist a finite-dimensional associative unital symmetric k-algebra A and a finite-dimensional unital left A-module M such that M is nonprojective and Ext^i_A(M, M) = 0 for every integer i > 0. By Corollary 1.2 and Proposition 8.1, for every field extension K/k the algebra A_K is symmetric and M_K is non-projective and self-orthogonal. The Lean formalization of the release covers the pair (A, M) over k; the field-extension, endomorphism-algebra and further homological consequences are outside its scope.

**Bridge.** H. Enomoto and R. Marczinzik, "On the homological conjectures for Artin algebras", arXiv:2609.19172 (right modules; ν_A = D Hom_A(−, A)). Proposition 2.3: let S be an algebra, V = S ⊕ W an S-module and B = End_S(V). If V is selforthogonal, then Ext^n_B(DB, B) ≅ Ext^n_S(ν_S V, V) for n ≥ 0; if V is also a cogenerator, then dom.dim B = ∞. Lemma 2.4: let V = S ⊕ W be a generator-cogenerator over S and B = End_S(V). If B is selfinjective, then V is injective; if S and B are selfinjective, then V is projective-injective. The Hochschild form of Tachikawa's conjecture is stated in Ringel's lecture notes "The homological conjectures".

**Deduction.** Take S = A^op, which is symmetric because A is, so that left A-modules are right S-modules. Let V = S ⊕ M and B = End_S(V) = End_A(A ⊕ M), with composition ab = a ∘ b; this is the opposite of the release's Γ, and the hypothesis and conclusion of Tachikawa's first conjecture are invariant under A ↔ A^op (apply the duality D; self-injectivity is left-right symmetric). V is selforthogonal: Ext^i(M, M) = 0 by the release, Ext^i(A, −) = 0 since A is projective, and Ext^i(−, A) = 0 since A is injective over the self-injective algebra S. As S is symmetric, ν_S is isomorphic to the identity functor, so Proposition 2.3 gives Ext^n_B(DB, B) ≅ Ext^n_S(V, V) = 0 for all n > 0. V is a generator-cogenerator, since S is self-injective and DS ≅ S. If B were self-injective, Lemma 2.4 would make V projective-injective, so its summand M would be projective, a contradiction. For K = k only Theorem 1.1 of the release is used; for K/k the base change of the release gives A_K symmetric and M_K non-projective with Ext^i_{A_K}(M_K, M_K) = K ⊗\_k Ext^i_A(M, M) = 0 (flatness), and the same argument applies. For the Hochschild form: Hom_{A^e}(X, A ⊗ A) ≅ Hom_A(X ⊗\_A DA, A) naturally, because A ⊗ A ≅ Hom_k(DA, A) as bimodules, and P_• ⊗\_A DA is a projective resolution of DA when P_• is a projective bimodule resolution of A; hence Ext^n_{A^e}(A, A^e) ≅ Ext^n_A(DA, A).

**Scope.** The statement concerns finite-dimensional algebras over the fields K containing 𝔽₂(q, H₁, H₂), including an algebraic closure of k.

## 5. Wall's question, the Kapovich–Kleiner conjecture and the toral relative Cannon conjecture

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "A Modulus Proof of Cannon's Conjecture" (family 246: every hyperbolic group with boundary homeomorphic to S² admits a proper cocompact isometric action on ℍ³ with finite kernel).

- (a) (Wall's question, hyperbolic case.) Every word-hyperbolic group that is a PD(3) group over ℤ (the orientation character may be nontrivial) is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold, in particular of a closed aspherical 3-manifold; the manifold may be nonorientable.
- (b) (Kapovich–Kleiner conjecture, torsion-free case.) Every torsion-free hyperbolic group whose Gromov boundary is a Sierpiński carpet is the fundamental group of a compact hyperbolic 3-manifold with nonempty totally geodesic boundary; equivalently, it acts properly and cocompactly by isometries on a convex subset of ℍ³ bounded by disjoint totally geodesic planes.
- (c) (Toral relative Cannon conjecture, Groves–Manning–Sisto Conjecture 1.3.) Let (G, P) be a relatively hyperbolic pair with P a nonempty finite collection of free abelian peripheral subgroups (necessarily of rank 2) and Bowditch boundary homeomorphic to S². Then G is Kleinian, i.e. isomorphic to a discrete subgroup of Isom(ℍ³).

**Release input.** Family 246, "A Modulus Proof of Cannon's Conjecture" (OpenAI, September 23, 2026), Theorem 1.1: "Let G be a hyperbolic group with boundary of G homeomorphic to S². There is a homomorphism ρ : G → Isom(ℍ³) for which the action on ℍ³ is proper and cocompact. Its kernel is finite." A finite kernel is allowed and the image may contain orientation-reversing isometries. Corollary 8.1: a torsion-free hyperbolic group with boundary S² is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold (possibly nonorientable). The Lean formalization of the release covers Theorem 1.1; Corollary 8.1 is outside its scope.

**Bridge.**
- (a) M. Bestvina, "Local homology properties of boundaries of groups", Michigan Math. J. 43 (1996) 123–139: Example 1.2(i) (a torsion-free hyperbolic group has a 𝒵-structure whose boundary is the Gromov boundary, by Bestvina–Mess, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 4 (1991) 469–481); Theorem 2.8 (if G is a Poincaré duality group of dimension n over L whose orientation character has finite image and Z is a boundary of G, then Z is a homology (n − 1)-sphere over L); Remark 2.9 (homology manifolds of dimension ≤ 2 are manifolds, by Wilder, so Z ≅ S^(n−1) for n ≤ 3). For torsion-free hyperbolic groups, the equivalence of Wall's question with the Cannon conjecture is due to Bestvina, building on Bestvina–Mess. Wall's question is Question G2, p. 391, of Wall, Homological group theory (1979): is every PD(3) group the fundamental group of a closed aspherical 3-manifold?
- (b) M. Kapovich and B. Kleiner, "Hyperbolic groups with low-dimensional boundary", Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 33 (2000) 647–669 (journal numbering): Theorem 5 (for a hyperbolic group G with Sierpiński carpet boundary, the double Ĝ of G along the collection of peripheral subgroups is hyperbolic, contains G as a quasiconvex subgroup, and has boundary S²), Corollary 12 (Ĝ is torsion-free if G is) and Corollary 13: "Let G be a torsion-free hyperbolic group with Sierpinski carpet boundary. Suppose either (A) Cannon's conjecture is true, or (B) every 3-dimensional Poincaré duality group with a nontrivial splitting is the fundamental group of a closed 3-manifold. Then G is the fundamental group of a compact hyperbolic 3-manifold with totally geodesic boundary."
- (c) D. Groves, J. F. Manning, A. Sisto, "Boundaries of Dehn fillings", Geom. Topol. 23 (2019) 2929–3002: Conjecture 1.3 (relative Cannon conjecture) and Corollary 1.4 (the Cannon conjecture implies the relative Cannon conjecture).

**Deduction.**
- (a) Let G be hyperbolic and PD(3) over ℤ. Poincaré duality groups have finite cohomological dimension, so G is torsion-free. By Example 1.2(i) the Gromov boundary Z is a boundary of G, and the orientation character takes values in {±1}. Theorem 2.8 with n = 3 and L = ℤ shows that Z is a homology 2-sphere, and Remark 2.9 gives Z ≅ S². Theorem 1.1 of the release provides ρ with a proper cocompact action and finite kernel. The kernel is a finite subgroup of the torsion-free group G, hence trivial, and point stabilizers of the proper action are finite, hence trivial. So ℍ³/ρ(G) is a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold with fundamental group G, as in Corollary 8.1 of the release.
- (b) Let G be torsion-free and hyperbolic with carpet boundary. By Theorem 5 and Corollary 12 of Kapovich–Kleiner, Ĝ is torsion-free and hyperbolic with boundary S². Corollary 8.1 of the release gives Ĝ = π₁(M) with M a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold; this is the step "if (A) holds, then Ĝ is the fundamental group of a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold" in the proof of Corollary 13. The remainder of that proof (Mostow rigidity for the reflection group acting on Ĝ by conjugation, convex cocompactness of G, convex hull bounded by totally geodesic planes) does not use Cannon's conjecture again, and Mostow rigidity applies to lattices in the full group Isom(ℍ³).
- (c) Groves–Manning–Sisto, Corollary 1.4, states that the Cannon conjecture implies the relative Cannon conjecture; the release theorem is the Cannon conjecture.

**Scope.** Part (a) concerns word-hyperbolic PD(3) groups over ℤ, part (b) is for torsion-free groups, and part (c) concerns pairs (G, P) with P a nonempty finite collection of free abelian peripheral subgroups of rank 2.

## 6. C∗-simplicity of Thompson's group T and further consequences of the nonamenability of F

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "Thompson's group F is nonamenable" (family 248: Thompson's group F is not amenable). Here F is the group of increasing piecewise linear homeomorphisms of [0,1] with finitely many pieces, dyadic breakpoints and slopes in 2^ℤ, and T is the group of orientation-preserving piecewise linear homeomorphisms of ℝ/ℤ with finitely many breakpoints, all dyadic, slopes in 2^ℤ, mapping dyadic rationals to dyadic rationals (Cannon–Floyd–Parry convention); F is the stabilizer of the point 0 in T.

## 11. The quantum Fourier transform and fanout in constant depth

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1 of the OpenAI paper "Product-projection localization and the QAC⁰ parity lower bound" (family 274: Parity is not in QAC⁰, measured-output model) and on Theorem 1.1 of Gretta–Joshi, arXiv:2608.06703 (which rests on Gretta–Gupta–Joshi, arXiv:2604.02793 and arXiv:2604.15298): the quantum Fourier transform cannot be approximated in QAC⁰, even to inverse-polynomial fidelity. Precisely, fix an integer d ≥ 0 and a real c ≥ 1; for each n let q_n be any modulus with 2^n ≤ q_n < 2^(n+1), where QFT_q|x⟩ = q^(−1/2) Σ_{y<q} e^(2πixy/q)|y⟩ for x ∈ {0, …, q − 1}, acting on the first ⌈log₂ q⌉ qubits of an (n + 1)-qubit register (q_n = 2^n is allowed). Then for all sufficiently large n there is no circuit of depth at most d on at most n^c qubits in total, built from arbitrary one-qubit gates and Toffoli gates of arbitrary arity (every layer has pairwise disjoint supports; no intermediate measurement, no postselection, no fanout gate), whose ancillas start in |0⟩ and are returned to |0⟩, and which implements a gate W on the register with |⟨ψ|QFT_q† W|ψ⟩|² ≥ n^(−c) for every unit vector ψ in span{|0⟩, …, |q − 1⟩}. In words: QFT_q is not in QAC⁰ in the approximate sense, for every q including q = 2^n, and fanout is necessary for the quantum Fourier transform in constant depth. This answers affirmatively the question of Fang, Fenner, Green, Homer and Zhang (2006): "is fanout really necessary to do the quantum Fourier transform in constant depth?".

**Release input.** Family 274, "Parity is not in QAC⁰". (a) "Product-projection localization and the QAC⁰ parity lower bound" (OpenAI, September 24, 2026), Theorem 1: fix a nonnegative integer d, a real number c ≥ 1 and 0 < ε ≤ 1/2; for all sufficiently large n, no circuit of depth at most d on n input qubits and at most n^c total qubits, using arbitrary one-qubit unitaries and unbounded-arity Toffoli gates, computes PARITY(x) with probability at least 1/2 + ε for every x ∈ {0,1}^n; ancillas start in |0⟩, and one output qubit is measured. Depth is the number of layers of pairwise disjoint gates, one-qubit gates included. (b) The companion paper "Regular trajectories, pruning and quantum parity" (OpenAI, September 24, 2026), Theorem 1.1, with its remark that H^(⊗(n+1)) F_n H^(⊗(n+1))|y, b⟩ = |y, b ⊕ PARITY_n(y)⟩ for the coherent fanout F_n|y, b⟩ = |y ⊕ b, b⟩. The Lean formalization of the release covers the parity statement (Theorem 1); Corollary 8 and the fanout reductions are outside its scope.

**Bridge.** Lucas Gretta and Malvika Raj Joshi, "Shor's algorithm requires Fanout", arXiv:2608.06703 (v3, 25 August 2026), Theorem 1.1: for any q ∈ ℕ and n = ⌈log₂ q⌉, if there exists a QAC⁰ circuit C approximating QFT_q to a non-negligible fidelity δ, then FANOUT_n ∈ QAC⁰. Here QFT_q|x⟩ = q^(−1/2) Σ_y ω_q^(xy)|y⟩ with ω_q = e^(2πi/q); the approximating gate W satisfies |⟨ψ|QFT_q† W|ψ⟩|² ≥ δ for all normalized ψ in span{|0⟩, …, |q − 1⟩}; in the proof, non-negligible means δ ≥ n^(−O(1)); FANOUT_n is the unitary with |0⟩\_t|0ⁿ⟩ ↦ |0⟩\_t|0ⁿ⟩ and |1⟩\_t|0ⁿ⟩ ↦ |1⟩\_t|1ⁿ⟩, and the generalized fanout follows from it by Rosenthal (2021). The note records that QFT_q ∈ QAC⁰ if and only if FANOUT_n ∈ QAC⁰, the converse direction being due to Høyer–Špalek together with Mosca–Zalka. The proof uses Gretta–Gupta–Joshi, "Parity not in QAC⁰ iff QAC⁰ is Fourier-concentrated", arXiv:2604.02793, Lemma 4.9 and Corollaries 4.5 and 4.10 (if an n-qubit state ρ with felinity F_n(ρ) = ε can be constructed by a depth-d circuit with m = poly(n) ancillas, then FANOUT_n is implemented exactly by a circuit of depth O(d) with Õ((m + n)/ε) ancillas), and Fact 3.9 and Corollary 3.16 of Gretta–Gupta–Joshi, arXiv:2604.15298 (exact amplitude amplification, after Grier–Morris–Wu).

**Deduction.**
- (1) Suppose that, for fixed d and c, a circuit C as in the statement exists for some q = q_n ∈ [2^n, 2^(n+1)) and infinitely many n. By Theorem 1.1 of Gretta–Joshi (product state ψ_q of Lemma 2.1, approximate Fourier states of Lemma 2.2, reflections, and the state ν_q on which W has n-qubit felinity Ω(δ²)) and Corollary 4.10 of Gretta–Gupta–Joshi, there is a QAC⁰ circuit of depth O(d) with poly(n) ancillas implementing FANOUT_n exactly, where n = ⌊log₂ q⌋ (the proof uses 2^n ≤ q < 2^(n+1)).
- (2) By Rosenthal's U, U† trick, the copy-form fanout gives the generalized fanout F_n with n extra ancillas and O(1) extra depth. Conjugating by Hadamard gates on all n + 1 qubits and using a zero target gives a circuit that computes PARITY_n(x) with probability 1 for every x; this is the remark of the companion paper (b).
- (3) Its depth is O(d) layers and its qubit count is polynomial in n. (Gretta–Joshi count only multi-qubit layers while the release counts all layers, a factor of at most 2D + 1, immaterial for fixed d.) For all large n this contradicts Theorem 1 of family 274 with ε = 1/2. Each reduction step is circuit-by-circuit with absolute depth constants, so the conclusion holds for all large n.
- (4) Hypotheses match: the direction used is the contrapositive of Theorem 1.1 of Gretta–Joshi (QFT_q approximately in QAC⁰ ⇒ FANOUT_n ∈ QAC⁰ ⇒ PARITY ∈ QAC⁰); the circuit models agree (arbitrary one-qubit gates, unbounded Toffoli gates or product-state reflections, disjoint layers, ancillas |0⟩, polynomially many qubits, no fanout gate, no intermediate measurement); the fidelity condition is that of Gretta–Joshi (worst case over the span, δ ≥ n^(−c)). The ancillas are returned to |0⟩ because the proof applies W as an operator on the register to ν_q and extracts amplitudes.

**Scope.** The statement concerns the transform QFT_q as defined above (not its inverse), for unitary circuits whose ancillas are returned to |0⟩.

## 12. The Strong Non-Synthesis Conjecture of Lombardi–Ma–Wright

**Statement.** Conditional on Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI paper "Polynomial-time unitary synthesis from a Boolean oracle" (family 283): the Strong Non-Synthesis Conjecture of Lombardi–Ma–Wright (LMW; STOC 2024, arXiv:2310.08870, Conjecture 1.5) is false. Precisely, let N = 2^n and K = K(n) = 2^⌊n/2⌋ (so K ≥ n^ω(1)). There is a uniform polynomial-size oracle circuit A (the circuit A_n of the release, followed by a classical test "is the n-qubit output among the first K basis states" and a measurement of one qubit), making polynomially many sequential queries, each of polynomial length, such that for every function family R : [K] × [N] → {+1, −1} there is a Boolean function f_R for which A^(f_R) wins the Oracle State Distinguishing Game on R with probability at least 3/4 − K/(2N), i.e. with distinguishing advantage (LMW Definition 3.12, Δ = 2|Pr[win] − 1/2|) at least 1/2 − 2^(−⌈n/2⌉). Hence the maximum distinguishing advantage Δ^avg_A (LMW Definition 3.13) is at least 1/2 − 2^(−⌈n/2⌉), which is not negligible, contradicting Conjecture 1.5: "For all K ≥ n^ω(1), any polynomial-query oracle algorithm A^f wins the Oracle State Distinguishing Game with advantage at most negl(n)". The same holds for any K ≤ N/4 (advantage ≥ 1/4). The release's error convention is used throughout: ‖Φ − U‖\_◇ = sup_ρ ‖((Φ − U) ⊗ id)(ρ)‖₁ with no factor 1/2, so that ‖Φ − U‖\_◇ ≤ 1/2 means a normalized diamond distance at most 1/4 in the convention of LMW.

**Release input.** Family 283, "Polynomial-time unitary synthesis from a Boolean oracle" (OpenAI, October 5, 2026), Theorem 1.1: "There are a polynomial p and a deterministic classical algorithm which, on input 1^n for any integer n ≥ 1, outputs in time at most p(n) a quantum oracle circuit A_n with the following properties. The circuit uses the gates H, T, T†, CNOT and a single Boolean oracle of input length m(n) ≤ p(n). Its total number of qubits, elementary gates, and oracle calls are each at most p(n). For every unitary U in U(2^n) there is a function f : {0,1}^(m(n)) → {0,1} for which the output channel Φ_{n,f} of A_n^(O_f) satisfies ‖Φ_{n,f} − U‖\_◇ ≤ 1/2." Conventions (Section 1.1): O_f|x, b⟩ = |x, b ⊕ f(x)⟩; the circuit depends only on n and the oracle depends on U, with no restriction on the truth-table size or classical circuit complexity of f; the output channel retains n designated qubits and traces out the rest. No Lean formalization is listed for family 283.

**Bridge.** Alex Lombardi, Fermi Ma, John Wright, "A one-query lower bound for unitary synthesis and breaking quantum cryptography", STOC 2024 (doi 10.1145/3618260.3649650), arXiv:2310.08870 (numbering of arXiv v1). Definition 1.3 (Oracle State Distinguishing Game) and Conjecture 1.5 (Strong Non-Synthesis Conjecture), quoted above. Formal version (Section 3): Definition 3.8 (the game: for b = 0 a random state |ψ_{R_k}⟩ with k uniform in [K]; for b = 1 a random basis state |x⟩, equivalent to a random binary phase state since E|ψ_h⟩⟨ψ_h| = I/N); Definition 3.10 (the adversary applies A^f, measures the first qubit and outputs it); Definition 3.12 (distinguishing advantage Δ_A(R|f) = 2|Pr[A^f wins] − 1/2|); Definition 3.13 (Δ_A(R) = max_f Δ_A(R|f) and Δ^avg_A = E_R Δ_A(R), so that f may depend on R); Remark 3.6 (a bit-flip query is simulated by one phase query). Section 1.1: any unitary mapping span{|ψ₁⟩, …, |ψ_K⟩} to span{|1⟩, …, |K⟩} would break the pseudorandom state family if it could be synthesized efficiently relative to f. LMW's diamond distance is normalized, D_◇ = max_ρ ½‖·‖₁.

**Deduction.** Fix n, N = 2^n, K = 2^⌊n/2⌋ and a function family R. Let S = span{|ψ_{R_k}⟩ : k ∈ [K]}, so dim S ≤ K, and choose a unitary U_R mapping S into T = span{|1⟩, …, |K⟩} (the unitary of LMW Section 1.1). Put M₀ = U_R† P_T U_R. Then ⟨ψ_{R_k}|M₀|ψ_{R_k}⟩ = 1 for all k, and for a random binary phase state ψ_h one has E_h ⟨ψ_h|M₀|ψ_h⟩ = Tr(M₀)/N = K/N. By Theorem 1.1 of the release there is f_R with ‖Φ_{n,f_R} − U_R‖\_◇ ≤ 1/2 in the unnormalized norm; for every input state σ and every 0 ≤ P ≤ I, |Tr P(Φ(σ) − U_R σ U_R†)| ≤ ½‖Φ(σ) − U_R σ U_R†‖₁ ≤ 1/4. The adversary A runs A_n^(O_f) on the challenge state, tests by an exact classical comparator whether the n-qubit output lies in [K], measures the result and outputs 0 if and only if it does. Then Pr[output 0 | b = 0] ≥ 3/4 and Pr[output 0 | b = 1] ≤ K/N + 1/4, so Δ_A(R|f_R) ≥ 1/2 − K/N and Pr[win] ≥ 3/4 − K/(2N); for K = 2^⌊n/2⌋, K/N = 2^(−⌈n/2⌉). Hypotheses match: (1) A is one circuit independent of R and the oracle f_R depends on R, as Definitions 3.10 and 3.13 allow; (2) A makes p(n) sequential queries of length m(n) ≤ p(n) and is uniformly generated; (3) a bit-flip query O_f equals one phase query on m(n) + 1 bits (Remark 3.6), so the query count is unchanged; (4) the factor-1/2 convention is as stated above; (5) K = 2^⌊n/2⌋ ≥ n^ω(1).

**Scope.** The statement concerns constant error, K ≤ N/4 and polynomially many sequential queries; it is consistent with the one-query results of LMW (Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 1.2/5.2), and LMW's Definition 3.16 (error 2^(−Ω(n))) and the setting K = N/2 are not part of it. Conjecture 1.5 is thereby unavailable as a route to security of the single-copy pseudorandom state family {|ψ_{R_k}⟩} of LMW Theorem 5.2, with n(λ) ≥ λ + 2 qubits, against polynomial-query oracle adversaries: that family is distinguished from a maximally mixed state with advantage at least 1/4 by a polynomial-query algorithm with an R-dependent oracle.
